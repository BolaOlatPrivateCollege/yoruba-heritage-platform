# Five-story illustrated and narrated editions — editorial production brief
**Àṣà & Ìtàn Yorùbá · private planning document · 23 November 2026**

## Editorial purpose
The public-facing library is a **collection of what is told**: oral traditions, regional variants, remembered heroes, living religious and cultural accounts, and newly written literary retellings. It does **not** certify one version of history. Be inviting and immersive. Keep source information accurate, subtle and accessible; never fabricate a teller, quote, interview, manuscript, age, location or permission.

The five manuscripts below are original literary compositions. Their titles, narratives and chapter counts are now used in the private six-chapter reader. **Visuals below are briefs, not completed or rights-cleared image assets. Narration scripts, audio and production permissions do not yet exist.**

## Experience specification for each story
- One original landscape hero illustration (16:9), plus a consistent 3:4 discovery thumbnail (cropped from composition only if appropriate). Add 1–2 optional chapter scene illustrations. No image should be treated as a photograph, ancient artifact or community documentary evidence.
- Captions: `Illustration for an original literary telling`. Alt text conveys visible action and characters, not spiritual interpretation.
- Yoruba proofreading: preserve ẹ, ọ, ṣ, tone accents and proper names; a native-proficient reviewer must resolve uncertain names or distinctions **before production**. Do not auto-strip diacritics.
- A clear, short **“This telling”** note beside the title: genre, which tradition inspired the story, what fictional elements were added, and what is *not* a source transcript. Long scholarly commentary belongs in Research, not between scenes.
- Audio, if later commissioned, begins with exact approved title and a short genre/attribution line. Use one consistent narration voice, naturally pronounce names, never imitate a specific elder or claim to be an eyewitness. Make chapter-by-chapter playback possible only after exact spoken transcript verification, rights and consent.
- Audio generation must be based on a **locked final manuscript**, not transient story drafts. Verify full duration, opening, last paragraph, six chapters, all names and complete playback from exported audio. Retain version metadata and the identity of the reviewer.
- Any future contribution feature is optional, private, moderated and consent-gated. **Not enabled now**.

## Story treatments

### 1 — Ìjàpá and the Feast Nobody Could Finish
**Manuscript:** `ijapa-feast-nobody-could-finish.md`; original trickster fiction.

**Cover art:** An expressive tortoise stands near an outdoor feast in an imagined Yorùbá-inspired village, holding a suspiciously oversized leaf while animals peer towards a row of half-hidden plates. Warm, playful atmosphere; gentle humour; no caricature of people or unsourced claim that the scene depicts a real ceremonial setting.

**Chapter stills:** the invented guest's message passed from household to household; Squirrel catching Ìjàpá near the leaf; Ìjàpá returning with a basket of vegetables.

**Voice direction:** wry, inviting àlọ́ cadence, quick comic pauses, no mocking tones for Yorùbá language. The ending is about restoring trust, not a sermon.

**Discussion prompt:** What is the difference between a clever tale and a lie told for personal gain?

### 2 — Mọ́remí and the Fire in the Reeds
**Manuscript:** `moremi-fire-in-the-reeds.md`; original dramatic retelling inspired by Ilé-Ifẹ̀ commemorative accounts.

**Cover art:** A determined woman walks beside tall rain-dark reeds toward a forest gate, distant lanterns and a fortified settlement in the background. Dramatic suspense without declaring invented clothing, architecture, ritual attire or specific raider identities historically authentic. Do not use a photograph of a real Ẹ̀dì event as if it depicts the imagined scenes.

**Chapter stills:** the marketplace of rumours; shadowy figures removing their leaf coverings at an ordinary camp; a quiet river at dawn after the city learns the truth. Avoid visually literalising violence or a child's sacrifice; the emotionally difficult cost can be portrayed through absence and stillness.

**Voice direction:** thoughtful suspense, hushed revelation, compassionate gravity in the final chapter. No performed “voice of Mọ́remí” claimed authentic.

**Discussion prompt:** Which matters most to the storyteller: how Mọ́remí discovered the secret, how the city responded, or the sorrow after the victory?

**Cultural review:** Ilé-Ifẹ̀ perspectives on Ẹ̀dì festival, historical enemy labels and river-vow variants before approving illustrations or adapting into other languages.

### 3 — Lísàbí and the Farmers' Promise
**Manuscript:** `lisabi-the-farmers-promise.md`; original historical-memory fiction inspired by Ẹ̀gbá traditions.

**Cover art:** A farmer sets an empty tribute basket beneath a broad village market tree while neighbours emerge from paths, each carrying work tools or produce. Emphasise communal agency, not a lone superhuman warlord. No specific costumes, military insignia or weapons labelled historically verified.

**Chapter stills:** a moonlit farmers' gathering with hoe handles; the once-empty tribute baskets arranged in the square; a child tracing three linked lines in soil.

**Voice direction:** tension rising slowly from everyday loss to a gathering movement; a strong, reflective last chapter. Do not treat alleged magical immunity as a witnessed fact or a practical protective technique.

**Discussion prompt:** In the versions you have heard, is the central hero Lísàbí, the people who followed him, or both?

**Cultural review:** local Ẹ̀gbá telling(s), locations, chronology and exact portrayal of tribute systems.

### 4 — Ṣàngó: When Thunder Answered
**Manuscript:** `sango-when-thunder-answered.md`; original framed fiction inspired by Ọ̀yọ́ royal memory and living Ṣàngó traditions.

**Cover art:** Rain clouds darken an imagined town square as a storyteller gestures beside a covered display holding a double-axe-shaped emblem. Human listeners of different ages gather as rain begins; the piece must visibly be **literary art**, not a documentation of sacred ritual. A real `òṣé Ṣàngó` should only be used from a licensed museum image with precise accession attribution.

**Chapter stills:** a royal courtyard with a young courier bringing bridge news; villagers carrying timber and rope; a visitor considering a double-headed axe carving after rain.

**Voice direction:** thunder is a soundscape transition, not a performance of ritual music or sacred chants. Do not invent liturgical formulae or borrow copyright-protected recordings. Avoid implying the king's imagined dialogue represents a canonical source.

**Discussion prompt:** What changes when a sacred name is heard through a royal tale, an object, and a family's belief?

**Cultural review:** practitioners' perspectives on emblems, Ọya/Ọ̀ṣun/Ọ̀bà as distinct sacred figures, and what should not be dramatised.

### 5 — Tìmì and Gbónká: The Duel That Would Not End
**Manuscript:** `timi-gbonka-two-champions.md`; original imaginative retelling inspired by rivalry motifs.

**Cover art:** Two champions face one another at a respectful distance on a dusty arena, a crowd behind them and smoke drifting between. Focus on the tension of choice rather than an impossible burning body. No claim this is a historically accurate event or a single canonical episode.

**Chapter stills:** an anxious royal court; Aṣàkẹ́ spilling a water jug during smoke and confusion; the champions standing side by side as the crowd reacts. No graphic injuries.

**Voice direction:** propulsive contest rhythm, pause for ambiguity after the smoky challenge, quiet uncertain ending. **Do not narrate magical feats as factual testimony** in credits.

**Discussion prompt:** What changes when the crowd demands a spectacular ending instead of listening to the champions?

## Asset and voice approval gates
1. Select original visual style suitable for the whole site, then generate **separate story-specific illustrations** only on editorial instruction.
2. Review image depictions for cultural sensitivity, anachronism and inadvertent claims of historical authenticity. Keep art credits/licences with each asset.
3. Complete Yorùbá orthography and community-tradition review before recording narration.
4. Lock final text version; generate audio separately from already preserved documentary Fola WAV assets. **Do not overwrite existing WAVs**, transcript hashes or archive links.
5. Perform human auditory QA against each approved text, then link audio only after explicit approval.
6. Continue to require owner-controlled manual release. No merge, deploy, publishing, collection of real community material or external hosting spend is authorised by this brief.
