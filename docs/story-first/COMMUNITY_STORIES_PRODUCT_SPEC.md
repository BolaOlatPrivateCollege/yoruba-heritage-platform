# Stories & Living Memories — reader-facing product blueprint

**Status:** private design draft · Owner direction 23 November 2026 · Companion to [Task 001L](https://github.com/BolaOlatPrivateCollege/yoruba-heritage-platform/issues/31) and [Task 001M](https://github.com/BolaOlatPrivateCollege/yoruba-heritage-platform/issues/32). No published feature, working form or real submissions are implied.

## Our promise to readers

*Come for the story. Stay for the mystery. Help us remember what another person might have forgotten.*

The learner library offers good tales **first**: humour, suspense, family memories, encounters, legends, competing accounts and communal imagination. The evidence is maintained carefully, but the public story must not read like an inventory of museum entries or an academic literature review. Readers can explore how different towns tell an account, ask questions, and contribute their version rather than being handed a rigid verdict. Even though younger readers are a priority, the writing must also respect adults and make them want the next chapter.

**Distinct space:** the pre-existing evidence-led documentaries and academic archive remain for scholars, teachers, historians and readers who want them. They are **not** deleted, reworded or substituted. The existing five original recorded Fola English tracks remain tied only to their unchanged documentary transcripts and require existing licensing/release gates.

## Proposed information architecture

### Main reader area — Stories & Living Memories

- **Story home:** carousel-free editorial grid of rich original covers and intriguing, honest hooks; browse *Folktales & Legends*, *Mysteries & Unanswered Questions*, *True Stories From the Past*, *Stories Told in Our Communities*, *Family Memories*, *Make & Play*, *Your Versions*, and *Latest Additions*. Classify source/genre clearly; avoid fake 'verified by elders' labels.
- **Story detail:** large readable narrative in short chapters, one word-help toggle, audio **only if it matches this exact version**, place/time only when known, personal/institutional credit with consent, one engaging question after the ending, and a community-voices invitation. Do not fill the middle with scholarly caveats.
- **Many voices:** if multiple communities tell variants, show **Version A from [town / storyteller if permissioned]**, **Version B from [town]** side-by-side as independent tellings rather than collapsing them into one authoritative composite. Explain respectful difference in a sentence if needed. For rumours, separate claims from confirmed history.
- **Classroom circle:** 2–3 easy discussion starters (*Who would you believe? Why did the narrator change the ending? What would you ask an elder?*) and 'Take a story home' for a supervised offline task.
- **Community contribution:** *Tell your version*, *Correct a detail*, *Share a memory*, *Ask a community question*. Prominent but never an intrusive modal.
- **Scholar bridge:** optional understated link labelled *Explore the documentary research* which leaves the storytelling area and opens distinct source-rich research pages. The main reading view **does not display research notes, footnote panels, museum object IDs or publication-status disclaimers**. Genre/provenance labels remain accessible to avoid blurring fiction and testimony.

### Separate researcher area — Documentary & Research

Maintain research documents, passage-level citations, contemporary scholarship, archaeological and object evidence, historical debate, provenance, language analysis and existing source safety. Learner content is never allowed to silently modify research content or attest that a community has endorsed it.

## Content types and editorial integrity

| Type | For the reader | What editors must have before publication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Folktale / legend** | A satisfying narrative; visible teller/community when permissioned | Named/permitted provenance or clearly disclosed adaptation |
| **Community oral history** | Someone's memory or an account passed down, as a story | Speaker/collector credit or agreed anonymity, consent, contextual dates and appropriate safety review |
| **Mystery / local rumour** | Several tellings and an open question; no sensational 'fact' title | Careful neutral status, no allegations about identifiable living people, moderator decision |
| **Historical adventure** | Well-paced factual events, with scenes supported or clearly framed | No unlabelled invented quotations, eyewitness scenes, motives or material events |
| **Original fiction** | Free creative narrative inspired by community interests | Very clear 'Original fictional story' label; no misrepresentation as documented tradition |
| **Correction / alternate telling** | An approved adjacent version, not a hostile comment thread | Submitter permission, review history, respectful contextualisation |

*Myth* is a traditional sacred/narrative category, **not** shorthand for false or foolish. No claims of esoteric rituals, fabricated oríkì, faux elderly interviews or unapproved translations of sacred content.

## Reader journey examples

**Mysterious story card:** *Who Was the Laughing Mask Talking About?* — 'By morning three people had three different answers. Nobody had asked the performer.' Type: **Original fictional learning tale inspired by living performance**, not an eyewitness account; location: **imagined town**. Action: **Read the tale**.

**Community version card:** *A story people tell about a river* — only after a named and consented community telling is collected. Title, locality, teller credit preference, retold/translated disclosure and version history; no fake pre-populated native testimony.

**After the ending:** 'Does your family or town tell a different version? Tell us what you heard, where it came from and whether we have permission to share it.' One optional question per story, not a research worksheet by default.

## Submission experience — built for trust

1. Reader chooses **Correction**, **Another version**, **My family's telling**, **A new story**, or **A question**.
2. Enter story and context: broad town/community, dialect if applicable, how heard (first-person / told by family or community / retold from reading / self-written fiction), approximate era, speaker or collector relation if permissioned, and freeform narrative.
3. Attach optional audio/image only with ownership, third-party permission and broad moderation safeguards. Show privacy terms before submission. Never invite a young person to provide full street address, phone number or details about a living neighbour's alleged misconduct.
4. Choose public credit: approved real name, pseudonym or anonymous; private contact only when necessary and with appropriate consent. Child submissions require a school or guardian-mediated approval path. Reading and classroom discussion require no public login.
5. Private editorial queue shows pending, needs information, held for specialist/local context, rejected for safety, approved attributed account, later corrected or withdrawn. The submitter can request correction/withdrawal; no automatic publication or endorsement.
6. A reviewed public addition appears under **Community voices** with its own type, place/era as appropriate, contributor consent and version identifier. Corrections transparently reference the original; contrary variants are preserved when safe.

No upload handling or public form may be activated until security/privacy infrastructure and moderation capacity exist. Content filters and youth safeguards must be independently reviewed, with clear data retention/withdrawal.

## Editorial roadmap — more than five essays

**First 12 episode candidates** (editorial leads, not pre-validated accounts):

1. *Who Was the Laughing Mask Talking About?* — original Gẹ̀lẹ̀dẹ́-inspired fictional mystery and an invitation to collect genuine versions.
2. *The Cloth With a Story Inside* — a maker, a market and disputed pattern names; obtain actual maker testimony before presenting it as real oral history.
3. *The City With More Than One Beginning* — attributed variants of Ilé-Ifẹ̀ beginnings; no universal claim.
4. *The Women Who Asked Where the Money Went* — dramatic, responsibly sourced events in Abẹ́òkúta; no invented real speeches.
5. *The Secret of a Blue Pattern* — practical Àdìrẹ craft story or original fictional teaching account.
6. *A River With Many Voices* — Ọ̀ṣun narratives with community-specific permission and respect for sacred contexts.
7. *When the Drum Was a Messenger* — a commissioned account from local performers; avoid fabricated drum meanings.
8. *The Market That Remembered* — contributions from traders on market changes and family memory.
9. *Why Three Towns Tell It Differently* — a series explicitly showing named variants with permission.
10. *The Night a Rumour Went Further Than the Truth* — original fiction on hearsay and misinformation, explicitly labelled.
11. *The Child Who Asked the Oldest Question* — original fiction introducing the work of community custodians and storytellers.
12. *What Was Left Out of the Famous Tale?* — corrections/adaptations from verified named local versions.

A second wave can follow places, foodways, craft, festivals, oral literature, civic struggles, Yoruba in the diaspora and lives of ordinary people. Avoid copying copyrighted written folktale collections into the platform.

## Engineering / rollout boundaries

- **Phase A (private editorial):** improve one genuinely immersive pilot story and mobile story-card concept. Solicit owner judgment on entertainment/readability. Build no public form.
- **Phase B (private contribution workflow):** design and review staff moderation queue, permission/credit/withdrawal model and supervised minor contribution path. Security review before collecting any actual data.
- **Phase C (pilot community intake):** invite identified community collaborators with explicit consent, record provenance and offer more than one version; independently review Yorùbá spelling, translation and cultural issues.
- **Phase D (private UI integration):** story library, versions, teacher circle and behind-the-scenes scholarly links. Test mobile 320/390/1440, no-JS, accessibility, escaping/untrusted text, forms/anti-spam and rights.
- **Phase E (owner approval):** only then consider separate public launch with robust editorial moderation.

Never change or merge Task 001J-A draft PR #30 or overwrite its five recorded original documentary narrations. No indexing, publishing, public data collection or deployment in Phase A.
