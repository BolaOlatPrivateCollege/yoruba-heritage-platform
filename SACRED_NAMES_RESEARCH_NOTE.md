# Olódùmarè & Òrìṣà — research note for Àṣà & Ìtàn

**Reviewed for preliminary publication: 22 November 2026**  
**Status:** Source-linked editorial research, not a final historical-linguistic reconstruction or approved speech from every Yoruba tradition.

## 1. The name **Olódùmarè**

**Religious referent:** In many Yoruba religious communities, Olódùmarè names the supreme divine source or ultimate authority. It is not normally used as a label for a specific specialised òrìṣà such as Ògún or Ọ̀ṣun. Prayers, proverbs and theological discourse support this contextual meaning. Religious identity and historical word formation are different questions.

**How the name was formed:** There is **no single universally accepted historical etymology** or proven record identifying the individual or time when the name was coined. A well-known proposal divides the written name into components approximating `Ol- / odù / marè`. In some accounts these elements are interpreted in terms of possession or supreme authority, greatness/fullness, and constancy/permanence. These associations are *proposed analyses*, not established morphology.

**Disagreement:** The linguist Ayo Bamgbose challenged simplistic segmentation, including treating the opening Ol- automatically as the possessor prefix in this particular name. David T. Adamo's published discussion compares interpretations attributed to earlier theologians and linguists. The underlying Bamgbose essay has been located bibliographically but **not fully read**, so the essay's nuanced argument should not be paraphrased beyond what is independently established.

**Variants and related names:** Olódùmarè, Òdùmarè and Edùmarè appear in scholarly discussions. Ọlọ́run (associated with ọ̀run, sky/heaven) and Ẹlẹ́dàá (Creator) may be used for the supreme divine referent in particular contexts, but one cannot assume complete theological equivalence or identical linguistic age. Devotional or oral interpretations concerning an inexhaustible source, a cosmic vessel or rainbow imagery require named versions and a verifiable original Yoruba text before quotation.

**Interreligious vocabulary:** The use of Yoruba divine names in Christian or Islamic contexts is a matter of linguistic and theological history. A shared designation does not establish that two theologies have always been the same. Published arguments over translating the biblical divine name into Yoruba are evidence of an ongoing interpretive debate, not proof of pre-Christian origins for a particular word division.

## 2. The name **Òrìṣà**

**Religious referent:** A broad indigenous sacred category frequently translated into English as divinity/deity. Actual roles and theological classifications differ across communities and diasporas. The label is neither a synonym for Satan nor an exact translation of angel, ancestor, or every spiritual force.

**How the word arose:** The historical origin is **not settled**. A widely repeated folk explanation associates `orí` (head, inner head/personhood) with `ṣà` (choose), yielding an interpretation about a “chosen head”. This may be meaningful to speakers or practitioners; however, it is **not a proven etymology**. Establishing a linguistic derivation requires consistent evidence about vowels, tones, intervening segments, older dialect forms, comparative phonology and historical attestations.

**Sacred proper name and general category:** Some lexical treatments propose a link between a high sacred proper name **Òrìṣà** and the broader term **òrìṣà**, sometimes discussed with Òrìṣàńlá and Ọbàtálá. This possible semantic history remains a research question. Òrìṣàńlá is commonly understood as “great òrìṣà”; the relationship between the titles and associated sacred figures must be explained with reference to named traditions.

**Dialect and diaspora:** Terms such as Ọ̀wọ̀ `Òrìchà`, Itsekiri `Òrìtsẹ̀`, Brazilian Portuguese `orixá` and English `orisha` illustrate variation and transmission. Similarity is a lead for research, not an automatic demonstration of who first coined a form or which language borrowed from another. The diaspora adaptations do not settle the original Yoruba word's earliest etymology.

**Do not collapse categories:** Orí is a major concept in personal destiny and personhood; irúnmọlẹ̀, ẹbọra and ancestral institutions have different traditions and potentially overlapping usage. A useful encyclopedia preserves the original term and the speaker's community rather than forcing a single rigid English pantheon.

## 3. Source strength and further tasks

1. **David T. Adamo (2019),** peer-reviewed discussion of Hebrew divine-name translation into Yoruba: [open article](https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?pid=S2305-08532019000100026&script=sci_arttext). The article compares **competing analyses** of Olódùmarè; its own theological recommendation should not be treated as universal consensus.
2. **Ayo Bamgbose (1971),** *The meaning of Olódùmarè: an etymology of the name of the Yoruba High God*: [bibliography](https://www.africabib.org/rec.php?RID=191712140). The full original research is **pending review**; bibliography is not full-text evidence.
3. **SAGE, Encyclopedia of African Religion,** [Olodumare](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/embed/africanreligion/chpt/olodumare): academic reference with a partial public excerpt, including its uncertainty about etymology.
4. **Indiana University Press,** [Òrìṣà: Spiritual Networks and Practices](https://publish.iupress.indiana.edu/read/global-yoruba-regional-and-diasporic-networks/section/8bbfb2c8-c763-4978-8c90-fe8310856004): scholarly contextual source for sacred categories; not a definitive historical derivation of the word.
5. **Wiktionary,** [òrìṣà lexical entry](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ori%E1%B9%A3a): records some spellings and popular explanations, but is collaborative lexical data, **not adequate by itself to verify reconstruction**.
6. **University of Alabama,** [Orí](https://globalcritical.barefield.ua.edu/self-and-persistence/ori/): discussion of the distinct philosophical concept; that alone does not establish `orí` as the historical root of `òrìṣà`.

A Yoruba historical linguist, Yoruba language academic, and willing traditional custodians from identifiable communities should review both pages. Record names, location, transcription, dialect, consent and variants of oral explanations. **Books by Samuel Johnson are excluded as factual authorities** under the owner's explicit instruction.
