# Community-led Historical and Cultural Editorial Policy

**Status:** Draft for review by Yoruba historians, linguists, artisans, religious custodians, medicine researchers and community representatives. Not a statement of any official council.

## Purpose

Àṣà & Ìtàn publishes historical, linguistic, social, artistic and scientific material about Yoruba-speaking communities and their connections across the world. It aims to foreground community knowledge **without equating every account with independently proven fact**. Regional, generational and religious disagreements should remain visible where relevant.

## Source-selection rule: Samuel Johnson excluded

At the project owner's instruction, **no statement on this website may be justified by the books of Samuel Johnson**, including *The History of the Yorubas* (published 1921, posthumously edited by Obadiah Johnson). Do not cite, paraphrase or present those books as evidence for origins, kingship, chronology, culture or beliefs.

- Prefer directly attributed testimony from consenting Yoruba community custodians; modern archaeology, linguistics, material culture and verifiable primary documents; and independently scrutinised scholarship.
- Check whether a secondary source merely repeats Johnson's version without independent evidence. If so, do not treat the repetition as corroboration. Attributions cannot be confidently traced through all external works without reading their full sources; record this limitation honestly.
- **Akinwumi Ogundiran, *The Yorùbá: A New History* (Indiana University Press, 2020)** is a priority reading lead. Its existence, scope, contents and scholarly reception are supported by the publisher and reviews; the website does **not** imply a page-level examination of the entire monograph. Add specific book claims only after verifying the passage and its evidence.
- Respect different town, royal, family and practitioner traditions without replacing disagreement with a universal narrative.

## Evidence classification for each claim

1. **Documented physical/historical evidence:** dated archaeology, inscriptions, source texts, museum provenance or verifiable records. State what has actually been measured or documented and what remains inferred.
2. **Recorded first-hand community account:** identify the narrator's preferred public attribution, community, approximate period, recorder, recording/transcription date, language and consent. An authentic statement proves that the person gave the account, **not necessarily that every event described happened exactly as narrated**.
3. **Inherited oral tradition:** preserve the account's family, shrine, town or performance setting. Do not fabricate dates or present genealogies as a fully verified timeline.
4. **Scholarly interpretation:** name the researcher, publication, page or reliable URL and note areas of disagreement. A summary or abstract is not the complete source.
5. **Religious or philosophical belief:** explicitly state whose belief it is. Avoid declaring paranormal causation as experimentally verified.
6. **Contemporary technical analogy:** a mathematical or design resemblance can support teaching and research without proving technological invention, priority or direct transmission.
7. **Unverified/needs review:** may remain a submission or research lead; do not publish as a confirmed historical claim.

## Submission intake checklist

- **Title and region:** identify the place, community and approximate date/period; avoid an unqualified “All Yoruba people do X.”
- **Attribution:** author, speaker, collector or custodian; indicate how they want to be credited. Are they a first-hand witness, retelling a family account or summarising a book?
- **Rights:** affirmative permission to quote or publish and ownership/licence of any media. Keep signed consent records outside the public repository.
- **Language:** record original Yorùbá words and dialect where possible, with a separately reviewed translation. Do not erase tone marks in educational content.
- **Corroboration:** search independent testimony, material evidence and written records; note disputes honestly.
- **Sensitive material:** do not publish addresses, children's identifying details, private family allegations, restricted rituals, confidential health information or culturally protected knowledge.
- **Scientific or medical claim:** distinguish ethnobotanical documentation from controlled evidence of safety and efficacy. No dosing instructions or claims of guaranteed cures.
- **Editorial record:** source IDs and links, reviewer's identity, date, corrections, outstanding questions, approval status and contact for rights requests.

## Moderation and discussion

All comments, discussions and source submissions begin **pending**. Approval confirms basic compliance with civility and publication standards; it does not certify historical truth. Consider an expert/custodian review for contested regional histories, sacred material, named third parties and high-stakes scientific or medical claims. Publish reasoned disagreements; reject targeted harassment, hate, scams, defamation, threats and unsupported medical promises. Allow a correction/withdrawal channel and an audit trail of substantive changes.

## Community custodianship

Not all knowledge is public. The presence of a ritual object, oral performance or family document online is not blanket permission to republish it. Contributors should be able to identify restricted information and decline disclosure. Negotiate benefits and licensing before commercial use. Include Yoruba-speaking communities beyond south-western Nigeria, and diaspora voices, without assuming one community speaks for the rest.

## Privacy and implementation

The current pilot stores submitted names, text and optional private contact details in a server-side SQLite database. Only approved threads and comments are returned to readers. Source submissions are editorial-only. Administrators must define lawful processing, retention, deletion, backups, security, incident response and a published privacy notice **before** launching publicly. The built-in server is not a comprehensive production-security solution.

## Sacred World editorial standard
- Separate Olódùmarè (supreme source), òrìṣà, regional figures, ancestors, ceremonies, and philosophical terms. Do not collapse all into “gods”.
- For every interpretation record speaker/community, location, approximate date, language, consent and whether the account is public, restricted or disputed.
- Make religious traditions understandable without declaring their supernatural content laboratory-proven. Objects and museum descriptions support claims about material culture, not the occurrence of miracles.
- Correct the equation of Èṣù with the Christian Satan while respecting Christians’ and Muslims’ ability to discuss their own theologies without abuse.
- Never allege that a named living person is an àjẹ́ or publicise potentially harmful identifying rumours. No dangerous ritual/medical instructions.
- A chapter is an editorial research draft until review by a competent Yoruba-speaking practitioner or qualified scholar with a documented review date. The review process may retain multiple well-attributed versions.

### Historic etymologies and oral accounts (2026-11-22 addition)
Distinguish the established sacred *reference* of a name from a conjectural *historical derivation*. The origins of **Olódùmarè** and **Òrìṣà** are not linguistically settled. Preserve differing local readings with named consented attribution. The popular `orí + ṣà` interpretation of òrìṣà is a folk analysis, not a proven linguistic origin. Published source metadata and catalogue records are not substitutes for examining full papers. Do not claim that Yoruba worshippers, scholars or translators agree universally on theological equivalences with Christianity or Islam.

### Bibliographic and philosophical sources (22 November 2026 update)
The Reading Centre includes three author dossiers and an explicit book-availability/status field. Sophie Olúwọlé’s **own philosophical analysis** may be cited with attribution, but her 2017 third-edition bibliography includes Samuel Johnson; do not use Johnson-dependent history as evidence. Wándé Abímbọ́lá’s and Ifáyẹmí Ẹlẹ́buìbọn’s volumes are important research leads; where only library records or publisher abstracts were inspected, no fabricated page quotation or claim of complete reading is permitted. For scientific claims, human welfare, witchcraft allegations, and religious comparisons, publish an evidence classification and identify who endorses the claim.
