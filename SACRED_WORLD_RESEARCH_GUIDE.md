# Sacred World — research and correction register

**Editorial version:** 22 November 2026 · **Status:** source-linked research drafts, not final community or peer review.

## The principle

A Yoruba religious term must be explained through Yoruba categories first, with geographic and community attribution. A sacred account is real evidence that a community teaches a belief; it does not automatically verify the event described. Scholars and custodians may disagree, and the archive must record disagreements rather than erase them.

This register explicitly excludes Samuel Johnson’s books as authorities. Secondary derivative dependence remains to be audited claim by claim. Most records below link to public museum pages, heritage inventories and academic articles; some links provide only abstracts or catalogue entries. **They cannot substantiate every sentence in the chapters.**

## Required community verification

- Invite **voluntary** input from Yoruba-speaking custodians and experienced practitioners for each named town/lineage. Record narrator, location, year, transcript language, orthography, usage rights, the date of consent and any restrictions.
- Get a separate reviewer for Yoruba language and phonology. Distinguish your editor’s interpretation from the custodian’s own words.
- Follow up on Èṣù-Ẹlẹ́gbára translation history with first-person public Ifá commentary and a specialist translation historian.
- Prioritise local oral histories of Òṣogbo/Ọ̀ṣun, Ìlọbú/Èrìnlẹ̀, Òwò/Olókun, Ìràwò/Ọ̀rìṣà Oko, Ọ̀yọ́/Ṣàngó, plus community-specific Yemọja and Odùduwà traditions. Do not declare these regions the exclusive origins of the figures.
- Ask practitioners which public differences across regions they want attributed. Restricted songs, rites and family records should be withheld.
- Verify medical, botanical and epidemiological claims with relevant scientific experts; respect traditional knowledge owners and do not publish formulas without rights and safety review.
