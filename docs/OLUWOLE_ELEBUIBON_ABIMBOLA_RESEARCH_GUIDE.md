# Reading dossier: Sophie Olúwọlé, Ifáyẹmí Ẹlẹ́buìbọn, Wándé Abímbọ́lá

**Project:** Àṣà & Ìtàn — Yorùbá Heritage Research Centre  
**Research review:** 22 November 2026  
**Status:** Annotated research dossier, *not a complete scholarly edition or peer review*.  
**Restriction:** Samuel Johnson's books are excluded as factual authorities, including indirect retellings unsupported by independent primary evidence.

## Why these authors matter together

These authors should not be collapsed into one undifferentiated account of Ifá. **Wándé Abímbọ́lá** is particularly important for scholarship, transcription, editing, translation, structure and analysis of Ifá oral texts. **Sophie Bọ́sẹ̀dé Olúwọlé** worked with the tradition to develop comparative philosophical arguments, especially around reason, ethics and complementarity. **Ifáyẹmí Ẹlẹ́buìbọn** writes from the lived viewpoint of a named Yoruba Ifá practitioner and oral performer. These approaches can illuminate one another but have different levels and kinds of authority.

## Sophie Olúwọlé — *Socrates and Ọ̀rúnmìlà*

- **Publication:** *Socrates and Ọ̀rúnmìlà: Two Patron Saints of Classical Philosophy*. Ark Publishers, Lagos. Indiana University Libraries catalogue: **2014**, 224 printed pages plus preliminaries. The 2017 digital copy named by the project owner calls itself the **third edition**, copyright 2017. Do not present 2017 as the first publication.
- **Research access:** The owner's Think Yoruba First PDF endpoint could not be fetched directly during this review. An indexed alternate text rendition identified as the 2017 third edition enabled targeted inspection of the title/front matter, table of contents, acknowledgements, philosophical passages and bibliography. The complete physical edition has not been collated page-by-page.
- **Key chapters in the 2017 table of contents:** three characterisations each of Socrates and Ọ̀rúnmìlà; what their voices are said to teach; concepts distinguishing African and Western philosophies; traditions as conceptual frameworks; binary complementarity and a postscript about social ethics.
- **Claim that can safely be used:** The author advances an argument that Yoruba Ifá oral literature contains material worthy of sustained philosophical analysis, and compares recurring concerns of ethical life with classical Greek treatments. Record **"Olúwọlé argues..."** rather than presenting her reconstruction as universally agreed history.
- **Important original acknowledgment:** She credited Abímbọ́lá's work on the sixteen principal odù for inspiring her work on Ifá philosophy. That is clear support for the connection between these authors.
- **Limits:** Her chronology and historicisation of Ọ̀rúnmìlà, including comparisons assigning him dates near Socrates, remain interpretive hypotheses, not archaeologically established biography. Binary **complementarity** in this book is a philosophical framework, *not* proof that modern computer engineering developed from Ifá signs. Her bibliography **does include Samuel Johnson's _The History of the Yorubas_**. Do not import historical claims reliant on that book. Do not imply the author herself endorsed the project's exclusion policy.
- **External scholarship:** Gail Presbey, “Sophie Olúwọlé's Major Contributions to African Philosophy,” *Hypatia* 35 (2020); Heinz Kimmerle, 2014 review; Björn Freter, 2024 academic appraisal. These are interpretive scholarship, not independently dated accounts of Ọ̀rúnmìlà’s life.
- **Sources:** [Indiana University catalogue](https://iucat.iu.edu/iub/14907622), [Think Yoruba First book listing](https://thinkyorubafirst.org/books/socrates-and-orunmila-two-patron-saints-of-classical-philosophy-2/), [Cambridge/Hypatia](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/hypatia/article/abs/sophie-oluwoles-major-contributions-to-african-philosophy/4CC0A123381A4EFE19BB6473E1FD9804), [2014 scholarly review](https://www.ajol.info/index.php/ft/article/download/111251/101036), [2024 APA academic appraisal](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.apaonline.org/resource/collection/950518C1-3421-484C-8153-CDA6ED737182/BlackExperienceV24n1.pdf).

**Source handling:** Treat an odù label and a recited *ẹsẹ̀* as separate records. A sixteenth-principal-odù book does not equal a complete publication of all variants under the 256 signs. Before publishing a quoted verse, record the exact source edition, page, odù, original Yoruba, performed/collected variant, translator, reviewer, rights and consent. Do not fabricate excerpts or fill gaps with AI-generated sacred text.

**Evidence limits:** The 256 signs and 2^8 combinatorial model are suitable for mathematical comparison. They **do not** prove a historical causal line to binary electronics or contemporary programming. For religious descriptions, attribute to the author/lineage; for history or archaeology, use independent evidence.

**Sources:** [Author CV](https://wandeabimbola.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Wande-Abimbola-CV.pdf), [French national-catalogue listing of _Sixteen Great Poems_](https://ccfr.bnf.fr/portailccfr/ark%3A/16871/00111435197), [1970s Ifá book record](https://books.google.com/books?id=SI4IAQAAIAAJ).

## Ifáyẹmí Ẹlẹ́buìbọn — identifiable practitioner and locally grounded teaching

**Two priority research tracks** are **Ọbàtálá as recounted by identified custodians** and **Ẹlẹ́yẹ/Àjẹ́ as multivalent social and religious traditions**. The site must avoid accusations of witchcraft against identifiable living people. A portrayal of the Mothers from one lineage must not be presented as uniform across Yorubaland.

## Publication decisions and pending reviews

1. **Current status of this integration:** Three author studies and fifteen research-reference records have been added to the site's source-linked Research Centre. They are introductions, not comprehensive editions of the books.
2. **Verification scope:** Bibliographic records, an author CV, scholarly articles and part of a full-text indexed 2017 edition were inspected. The remaining books were not read in full, and publisher/library records **cannot** certify their contents. Do not invent chapter citations.
3. **Copyright and respect:** Do not reproduce copyrighted books or substantial sacred verse collections without clear permission. Link to catalogues, cite short quotations when lawful, and commission authorised recordings/translations from named custodians.
4. **Source contamination check:** *Socrates and Ọ̀rúnmìlà* references Samuel Johnson. We may cite Olúwọlé for **her own argument**, not treat Johnson-derived chronology or history as evidence. All future books must undergo a source-of-source audit before historical claims enter the public site.
5. **Community validation needed:** Ask Ifá practitioners and Yoruba-language specialists to check any odù identifiers, Yoruba text and tone marks and to document regional variants. Academic reviewers should assess history and contested claims independently.
6. **Next engineering pass:** structured `Author`, `Work`, `Edition`, `Claim`, `SourcePassage`, `Reviewer`, `Permission` and `CommunityAccount` records in the production content model; link each article section to explicit pages where available.
