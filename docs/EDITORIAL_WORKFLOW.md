# Content taxonomy and editorial workflow

## First-level collections (proposed)
1. Origins, archaeology and historiography
2. Regions, towns, kingdoms and migrations
3. Language, alphabet, tone, literature and oríkì
4. Sacred traditions, Ifá and òrìṣà (community-specific)
5. Philosophy, ethics, metaphysics and mysticism
6. Marriage, kinship, lineage, family and rites of passage
7. Governance, justice, diplomacy and political institutions
8. Indigenous education, apprenticeship and pedagogy
9. Mathematics, numerals, timekeeping and observational knowledge
10. Medicine, healing systems, plants and public-health comparisons
11. Agriculture, ecology, food systems and environment
12. Technology, materials, metallurgy, textiles and architectural practices
13. Music, dance, drama, visual arts, festivals and oral performance
14. Trade, labour, markets, travel and economic history
15. Wars, enslavement, colonial encounters and independence
16. Diaspora and transatlantic Yoruba heritage
17. Modern science, innovation, entrepreneurship and global influence
18. Encyclopaedia resources: glossary, maps, timelines, interviews and bibliography

These collections must be extensible and allow regional sub-categories; never imply a single homogeneous Yoruba practice. Each section should cover historical context, local variations, primary/secondary evidence, current practices, and cautions where evidence is lacking.

## Article metadata requirements
`id`, `slug`, `title`, `language`, `collection`, `abstract`, `body`, `regions`, `community_provenance`, `source_ids`, `evidence_basis` (oral tradition / written record / archaeology / linguistics / artistic practice / scientific study), `verification_status` (unreviewed / reviewed / contested / needs-corroboration), `editorial_status` (draft / under_review / published / withdrawn), `reviewed_by`, `rights`, `published_at`, `updated_at`, and `version`.

## Sources
Canonical source metadata: title, author/organisation, publication year, work type, URL/identifier/ISBN, original language, relevant page or archive item when actually inspected, access date, evidence limits, Johnson-dependency check and permission for media reuse. Do not fabricate page ranges or direct quotations.

## Community testimony intake
Record narrated community (not just ethnic label); narrator/permission authority; interviewer; recording date; original language; public attribution preference; recording rights; embargo/restrictions; location precision safe for publication; transcript plus translation review; cross-references and disagreements. Participation must never require disclosure of secret or restricted cultural knowledge.

## Sensitive areas
- **Religion/òrìṣà:** distinguish emic (practitioner) accounts and academic interpretation; do not present one lineage as authoritative for all.
- **Science/mathematics/innovation:** interpret conceptual resemblance as a comparison, not proof of causal invention; refer to external evidence where applicable.
- **Healing/medicine:** clearly identify ethnobotanical reports versus verified clinical effects; avoid diagnosis, dosing and unsafe remedial instructions.
- **War, slavery, gender, identity:** use precise dates, geographical scope, multiple sources and a correction right.
- **Samuel Johnson exclusion:** no books by Samuel Johnson are acceptable as evidentiary sources. Trace suspect narratives in secondary references before publishing.

## Editorial workflow
`received -> rights/privacy check -> evidence review -> regional/custodian review when appropriate -> editor decision -> published -> amendments/corrections`.
A comment approval is not evidence verification; separate states must be stored.
