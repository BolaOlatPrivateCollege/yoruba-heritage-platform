# Mystery & Legends research leads — private editorial dossier

**Prepared:** 23 November 2026. **Status:** Source-researched editorial leads only, **not** approved adaptations, authenticated fieldwork, copyrighted republication, or proof of supernatural events. The website is for people of every generation, not a school portal.

## Principles
- Honour named Yorùbá tellers, families and communities, and preserve local variants as distinct tellings; do not invent a village elder or claim real interviews that never occurred.
- Distinguish folk narrative, original imaginative fiction, *modern literary work*, and viral mystery story. Sacred accounts are described from attributed practitioner/community viewpoints, without spiritual verdicts.
- No Samuel Johnson books or derivative Johnson accounts as historical sources.
- Where specific published wording, plots, dialogue, characters or sequences belong to modern authors, **link and attribute**; obtain republication/adaptation licences where required. Do not disguise a close paraphrase as original writing.
- These leads are independent of the first five ORIGINAL story manuscripts. Do not silently replace original scripts or archive history records.

## 1. Araromi-Oke — the alleged 1957 vanishing village

**Reader-facing classification:** *A modern circulated mystery / disputed legend*, not a verified 1957 historical incident or authenticated ancient Yoruba oral tradition.

**Claim under investigation:** Some recent internet versions say a settlement vanished in 1957 with food still cooking, property untouched and sometimes a goat left behind. These details are **story motifs**, not corroborated forensic observations.

**Verification:**
- Pulse Nigeria fact-check (18 Aug 2025) finds no credible historical record of a whole settlement's disappearance in 1957 and notes confusion among numerous Araromi-named places: https://www.pulse.ng/story/araromi-oke-disappearance-2025081810302546679
- Ekiti State Government **official town list** includes Araromi Oke Ekiti: https://www.ekitistate.gov.ng/about-ekiti/towns-villages
- Ekiti East LGA government page names Araromi Oke Ekiti and its quarters: https://www.ekitistate.gov.ng/about-ekiti/local-government/ekiti-east
- Leadership's field-reported article interviews traditional/local figures and questions the story's alleged place and event: https://leadership.ng/elders-traditional-head-debunk-fabled-disappearance-of-araromi-oke/
- A different online account asserts the viral imaginary/disappeared settlement may be a different place with the same name; this is an **unverified hypothesis**, not proof: https://historicalnigeria.com/the-vanishing-of-araromi-oke-myth-or-memory/

**Critical geographical correction:** Do not repeat the supplied “Ogun, Ekiti, Osun border” as verified geography. Viral accounts vary between Oyo/Osun/Ekiti, and **Araromi Oke Ekiti** is administratively in Ekiti East. The same or similar settlement names are not proof of identical locations.

**Potential original reader episode:** *The Village Everyone Remembered Differently* — two fictional oral-history collectors compare contradictory reports while a real living community's identity is respected. Use a fictional village name for any scene of mass disappearance unless permissioned testimony substantiates a concrete local telling. The user-facing intro should plainly say the 1957 event is unverified. Keep historical claims and source disagreement in Research rather than interrupting the narrative.

**Required before treatment as a local legend:** seek direct attributable, consented oral versions from named Araromi community custodians; preserve recorded place, date and informant metadata securely.

## 2. The Leopard-Man — a collected Yoruba folktale

**Classification:** Documented published literary transcription/collection of Yorùbá legend (not newly verified eyewitness testimony).

A full version appears as Chapter XII “The Leopard-Man” in *Yoruba Legends* associated with M. I. Ogumefu:
- https://sacred-texts.com/book/yoruba-legends/shell/xii-the-leopard-man
- https://tianmu.org/good-work-library/african/yoruba-and-ife/yoruba-legends
- A 2022 reading links the published account to M. I. Ogumefu (1929): https://sandmanstoriespresents.wordpress.com/2022/04/18/ep-123-the-leopard-man-and-other-yoruba-legends-m-i-ogumefu-1929/

The published version features a stranger who returns after about a month, a young woman who follows him and a leopard transformation. Exact collection attribution, collection date, editorial intervention, copyright status of any digital version and source community all require confirmation. A web copy may not identify the originating Yoruba storyteller or place.

**Suitable next step:** write a new folktale-context essay comparing published tellings, or obtain permission and adapt with transparent source credit and substantial independent dialogue, setting and scenes. Do not call Ogumefu's literary version a direct transcript of a named contemporary teller.

## 3. Olailo and the Mysterious Woman

**Classification:** Published contemporary telling of a folktale, with identifiable publisher/rights concerns.

Primary website:
https://yorubatales.com/olailo-and-the-mysterious-woman/

The published story follows a hoe maker/seller, an otherworldly woman, danger beyond familiar boundaries and his deceased mother's protective intervention. The page calls itself “Olailo and the Mysterious Woman” and identifies the setting as Kasawa. The site's copyright/author/licence and any traditional provenance must be checked before reuse.

**Suitable next step:** link to the full original, request adaptation permission and a narrator/collector provenance note. Could develop an **independently plotted** cautionary supernatural story about a craftsperson whose promise leads across a boundary, but not copy this exact ordered plot, names, dialogue, ten-headed antagonist or resurrection device under a new title.

## 4. “Ori (A Yoruba Folktale)” — Gbemisola Akinola-Alli

**Classification:** A *signed contemporary literary work*, **not** anonymous public-domain folklore. Do not categorise as an unattested communal oral transcript.

Primary publication: *Isele Magazine*, 30 April 2022:
https://iselemagazine.com/2022/04/30/ori-a-yoruba-folktale-gbemisola-akinola-alli/

The published fiction features Jumoke, her sister, a suitor, envy and supernatural impersonation. All story-specific characters, arrangement, dialogue and expression are subject to the author's rights, regardless of folk motifs.

**Suitable next step:** a short recommendation/review linking the author and publication; seek written adaptation permission for an illustrated or audio retelling. Independently create new work around broad themes such as identity and envy only with a wholly different plot.

## 5. D. O. Fágúnwà — mystical literary worlds, not eyewitness history

**Classification:** Author profile, literary history and critical appreciation of fictional supernatural adventure, **not** an unverified literal report of forest spirits.

Prefer specialised/institutional resources:
- Fagunwa Study Group profile: https://fagunwastudygroup.org/about-d-o-fagunwa/
- African Studies Centre Leiden literary overview: https://www.ascleiden.nl/content/library-weekly/daniel-o-fagunwa
- Family commemoration report from TheCable: https://lifestyle.thecable.ng/d-o-fagunwa-family-anniversary/
- A report based on family account: https://newsverge.com/2016/12/13/family-marks-53-years-passage-of-foremost-yoruba-author-fagunwa/
- User-provided essay, **secondary cultural commentary** featuring *speculative* death stories: https://medium.com/@Ayomidejoe/the-fagunwa-d-fagunwa-e4d610c422be

Fágúnwà was a pioneering Yorùbá novelist known for Ògbójú Ọdẹ nínú Igbó Irúnmọlẹ̀ and other fantastical adventures. Accounts from family commemorations report his accidental drowning near Bida in **December 1963**. Claims that a fictional monster such as Òjòlá-Ìbínú caused his death belong to speculation about a fictional character, not biographical fact. Sources differ in details about the river/location; establish exact locality from family records before publication.

**Copyright:** Nigeria's copyright guidance commonly states literary works are protected for the author's lifetime plus 70 years. As Fágúnwà died in 1963, **do not assume novels or modern translations may be freely copied, narrated or adapted in 2026**. See https://www.eregistration.copyright.gov.ng/ncc/faq and 2023 legislation https://www.copyright.gov.ng/CopyrightAct/CopyrightAct2023FinalPublication1.pdf . Translation and artwork carry additional rights.

**Suitable next step:** an original multimedia *Meet Fágúnwà* author profile with properly licensed cover images and short attributed synopsis, followed by an original fantasy serial with its own plot, characters and invented creatures—not a rewrite of protected novels.
